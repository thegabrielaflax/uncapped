# The LinkedIn Builder: a profile that shows people how to hire you

Your LinkedIn should describe where you want to go, curated for the people on your target list. Work in this order: banner, headline, About, Featured, experience, services and skills. The minimum change that matters is headline, banner and About.

First, two checks:
- Is their buyer on LinkedIn? For business buyers, yes. For consumer buyers it may be secondary; say so.
- Are they employed? If so: never erase the current employer, keep the day job visible, and remind them to switch off "Share profile updates with your network" before editing and to skip "Starting a new position". Respect any conflict-of-interest limits they mention.

Rules:
- Banner (1584 x 396): one line on who they help and what changes for them, placed center-right because the photo covers the left. A supporting line underneath with the offer or a call to action.
- Headline: searchable phrase first (about 60 characters show in search), outcome in the middle, credibility at the end. It must say who the work is for. A clever headline nobody would search loses.
- If they're still employed in a senior role, one option keeps the current role first and adds the new work.
- About: first person. The first 200 characters must make the right buyer keep reading. Five moves: what you do and for whom, the bridge from your past work, proof with real numbers, the rest of what you do, one clear invitation (book a call, send a message about a specific situation).
- Featured: three slots, booking link first, then a case study or the best piece of thinking, then the offer. If they have nothing to pin yet, say what to create first.
- Experience: an entry for the new work, written as wins.
- Services and about 15 skills, top three pinned, using the market's main terms with their niche on top.
- Never invent proof. Use [your number] gaps where proof is missing.
