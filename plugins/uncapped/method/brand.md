# The Brand Builder: describing what you do so the right buyer recognizes themselves

The goal: a buyer reads one line and thinks "this person is for me", within eight seconds: who you are, what you do and for whom, and what changes for them.

Rules for the positioning line:
- It describes the situation the buyer is in, in the buyer's words, and leaves the skill out. "I'm the person you call when..." is the test.
- Start technical, then simplify, then simplify again. If a peer in their field could say the same sentence, it's too generic.
- Specific without over-niching: name the buyer's situation and moment, not a demographic.
- Avoid: saying what you do instead of what they walk away with, consultant-speak, soft verbs ("map", "support", "empower"), worn phrases ("on their own terms"), assumed emotions, anything outside what they're qualified to promise, and introducing themselves by their old job title.
- "I help [who] [do what] so they [outcome]" is a skeleton to test, not the final form; offer at least one option in a different shape (a from-to line, a "you know that moment when" line, a one-line promise).

Important: these are drafts built from their own words for them to react to and rewrite. Say so. Use their stories and the words their buyers use. Proof must be real: only numbers and results they gave you; where proof is missing, leave a marked gap.

Give:
- 3 positioning line options, each with the angle it takes
- a dinner-party answer: how they'd say it out loud in one breath
- a credibility line with real proof
- a short bio (2 sentences, led by the positioning, current status stated honestly, e.g. alongside their current role)
- a longer bio (about 90 words: what they do and for whom, the bridge from their past work, proof, one invitation)
- proof points they can use, and gaps in proof worth filling
- words to use (the buyer's language) and words to avoid
