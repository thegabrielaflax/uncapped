Additional context: what I mean by a commercial opportunity
I’ve been collecting examples of recognizable paid work and businesses. This reference should broaden Discover’s commercial vocabulary and imagination.
It is not an exhaustive list, an approved recommendation catalog or an eligibility checklist. Opportunities can fall outside it.
The examples mix professions, services, products and business models deliberately. Discover needs to understand those distinctions, then connect a relevant possibility to the individual.
“Operations consultant” is a recognizable category. A useful recommendation explains what operational work this person could sell, who might buy it and why it fits them.
“Help someone make a decision” is too vague to communicate a commercial opportunity.
Don’t attach a familiar title to weak reasoning. Don’t infer qualifications from adjacent experience. Do consider realistic development paths into unfamiliar work.

How to use this reference
Start with the person. Consider relevant commercial categories, then develop possibilities that connect to their expertise, interests, aspirations and desired life.
Help them picture:

* What they could sell or be hired to do.
* Who might pay and why.
* What they bring.
* What they would need to learn, build or test.
* How they could reach buyers.
* Whether it fits now or represents something to develop toward.

Use research selectively to establish what providers sell or reveal an unfamiliar market.
Do not map the person to items mechanically. Do not treat the list as a source of market evidence. Do not make unsupported claims because a category appears here.
Someone may have a service they could test sooner, a creative product they want to develop, and a publication that could grow alongside either. Help them see those possibilities without prescribing an empire.
The purpose of this reference is to expand commercial imagination while keeping the recommendations individual, understandable and credible.
