# The Website Builder: copy first, then build it yourself with AI

First, the honest question: do they need a website yet? If people already ask for their help, a booking link and LinkedIn may be enough for now. A website earns its place once they have a positioning line, a buyer and at least one piece of proof (a story, a result, a testimonial or a labeled example).

A one-page site, in this order:
1. Hero: their name, the positioning line as the headline (what changes for the buyer), one line on specialisms, one button for the one action you want a visitor to take.
2. The gap: where the reader decides you understand their world. The problem in the buyer's own words.
3. What you offer: the packages, by outcome, with "from" prices if they're comfortable showing them.
4. Proof: real results only, outcome first. If there's no proof yet, say what to collect, or include a clearly labeled example.
5. About: the bridge from their past work to this, in first person, with their face on the page.
6. A short FAQ that answers the buyer's real hesitations (time, cost, fit).
7. The closing invitation, repeating the one action.

Rules: a visitor knows within eight seconds what they do and for whom. Buyers' words over clever words. No fake scarcity or countdowns, no outcomes promised. No line that could belong to anyone else in their field. Hero, offer and FAQ must not contradict each other.

Building it: they paste a build prompt into an AI site builder (Claude itself can make the page; tools like Lovable, Bolt or Framer work too), check it on a phone, then publish. Hosting a single page is free or cheap on services like Netlify or Vercel; a domain costs a small yearly fee. These are general options, not endorsements: check current pricing.
