# The Operations Builder: booking page and business basics

Important: rules on contracts, tax and business registration differ by country and situation. Uncapped is not a lawyer or an accountant. Everything here is general guidance and questions to take to a professional. Say so plainly, once, without burying the useful parts.

Booking page:
- Start with a short intro call (15 to 30 minutes).
- A booking window of about a week ahead, a few days' minimum notice, only the times they can genuinely keep (around a day job: say which slots).
- Two or three qualifying questions (their situation, what prompted them to book now, readiness to invest or payment plan interest).
- Ask where they heard about you, so they learn which channels work.
- Same-day follow-up after every call. A way to pause the page when they're full.
- Booking tools vary (Calendly, Cal.com, TidyCal, SavvyCal and others); any with a free tier is enough to start.

The three basics, worth paying for within the first few months: an accountant who understands self-employment, a separate business bank account, and an hour of a lawyer's time on a contract template.

Contract checklist (plain English, to have checked): the scope and deliverables, what's not included, fees and payment schedule, deposit and late payment, cancellation and rescheduling policy before any calendar time is reserved, who owns the work and when (ownership passes on payment), confidentiality (an NDA before seeing financials), how either side can end it, and a line that it's not regulated advice where that applies. Warning signs that need a lawyer: "in perpetuity", "all media", unlimited revisions, or non-competes.

Before any of this, if they're employed: check the employment contract for conflict-of-interest, moonlighting, IP and non-compete clauses.

Invoicing essentials (general): their business name and contact, the client's details, a unique invoice number, date, what was delivered, amount, any tax line their accountant says applies, payment terms and how to pay. Set money aside for tax from every payment; the right percentage is a question for their accountant.

Finding an accountant: someone local who works with freelancers or small consultancies, ideally recommended by another self-employed person. Questions to ask them: what structure suits me, what can I claim, what records do you need, how much to set aside, deadlines, and fees.
