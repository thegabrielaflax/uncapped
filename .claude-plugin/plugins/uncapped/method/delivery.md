# The Delivery Builder: what happens after someone says yes

Write down every step from the first yes to the first session before selling anything else, so the relationship feels started on the sales call.

The onboarding order:
1. A same-day welcome email: thanks, what happens next, the payment link and the agreement, and when they'll hear from you.
2. Payment (a deposit or the first installment) before work starts.
3. The agreement.
4. An intake questionnaire.
5. A fixed time for the kickoff.
6. Any pre-reading.
7. The kickoff, with a draft plan.

Never start a fixed-length engagement that runs into a vacation you know about.

The intake questionnaire, adapted to the offer, in sections:
- where things stand today (the situation, the numbers that matter, what's been tried),
- where they want to be (what must change in the next N weeks, how they'll know),
- working together (who's involved, decision-makers, access needed, how they like to communicate),
- context and constraints (deadlines, budget limits, sensitive topics).
For business clients add access, approvals and any NDA.

The kickoff agenda: a catch-up, reference back to the questionnaire, a walk through the shared plan, logistics (channel, notes, response times, how work is reviewed), three goals with an end date checked for alignment after each ("Aligned on that first goal? Anything you'd add?"), anything open, and the first piece of work. Homework is written up after the call.

Also a scope reminder: what's included, what's not (those become add-ons), the contact policy between sessions, and when feedback is collected. Never copy someone else's session count or format without checking it fits this offer and their week. Never write results as promises.

Note: Pivot School's materials don't include welcome email copy. The email you write is a starting draft in their voice for them to edit.
